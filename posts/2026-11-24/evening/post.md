Today in AI & Tech 🔥
Here are the top stories shaping engineering and development today.

🔹 OpenAI and Cisco Releases Shirudo: An Open-Source Linux Runtime Allowing Agents to Defend Systems Without Downtime
 ↳ What happened: OpenAI and Cisco have jointly released Shirudo, an open-source eBPF runtime for Linux that lets AI agents write and deploy signed "shields" to block active exploit paths on running systems instantly without needing downtime.
 ↳ Why it matters: This bridges the gap between fast-moving AI security solutions and the rigorous stability requirements of production Linux environments.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/24/meta-facebook-your-algorithm-llm-recommendations/

🔹 GPT-6 models demonstrate intrinsic protein-design capabilities
 ↳ What happened: OpenAI's latest model tests reveal that GPT-6 can autonomously design complex protein structures and sequences using integrated reasoning and generated code, successfully showing molecular binding across multiple target assays in the lab.
 ↳ Why it matters: Moving large language models from text generation into reliable molecular biology tools unlocks massive automation potential for biotech R&D.
 ↳ Read story: https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-models-demonstrate-intrinsic-protein-design-capabilities/

🔹 Tidal will start banning AI-generated music next month
 ↳ What happened: Streaming platform Tidal has announced a strict ban on AI-generated tracks starting December 9th, preparing to remove nearly 29 million automated songs from its catalog and block up to 85,000 new AI uploads daily.
 ↳ Why it matters: Content platforms are drawing clear governance boundaries around generative media to protect copyright and human artist revenue streams.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1041362/tidal-ai-music-ban

My take:
 ↳ We are rapidly shifting from passive AI chat tools to active system defenders and automated creators—which means robust platform guardrails and runtime security are more critical than ever.
