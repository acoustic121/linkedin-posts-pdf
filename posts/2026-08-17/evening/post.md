Today in AI & Tech 🔥
AI agents get open-source harnesses, safety teams get reorganized, and AI desktop apps start tracking keystrokes for automated workflows!

🔹 DeepSeek Launches Open-Source Agent Harness
 ↳ What happened: DeepSeek released DeepSeek Harness v0.1 in developer preview. It is an MIT-licensed framework where every agent capability is treated as a plugin, complete with session logging and provider-agnostic model routing.
 ↳ Why it matters: Gives developers total control to build modular AI agents without locking themselves into a single model provider.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/17/deepseek-ai-releases-deepseek-harness-in-developer-preview/

🔹 OpenAI Reportedly Disbands Preparedness Team
 ↳ What happened: Reports indicate OpenAI dissolved its preparedness group, which was tasked with assessing catastrophic risks from advanced models. Safety duties are likely being absorbed directly into general engineering groups.
 ↳ Why it matters: Signals a shift towards integrating safety into production engineering rather than keeping it in isolated teams.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/980817/openai-disbands-preparedness-team

🔹 ChatGPT App Introduces "Computer History" Action Tracking
 ↳ What happened: OpenAI added a Computer History feature to its ChatGPT macOS app. It tracks user clicks and keystrokes to learn workflows, suggest automations, and automatically finish repetitive desktop tasks.
 ↳ Why it matters: Developers get powerful contextual automations, but security teams must monitor what local data gets exposed.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/980742/chatgpts-computer-history-tracks-your-clicks-and-keystrokes

My take:
 ↳ The line between operational tooling and AI assistants is blurring rapidly. Whether building modular agent frameworks or automating local workflows, control over data privacy and system architecture remains critical for infrastructure engineers.
