Today in AI & Tech 🔥
From massive multi-agent scaling to reality checks on AI ROI, here are today's top updates!

🔹 Microsoft Open-Sources Agensh for Scaling 1,024 AI Agents
 ↳ What happened: Microsoft Research released Agensh, a multi-agent framework designed to scale up to 1,024 autonomous agents without a central orchestrator. Instead of relying on a master controller, agents self-organize using a shared Git repository, team chat, and a unified findings board.
 ↳ Why it matters: Decentralized orchestration simplifies building massive agent networks without creating single points of failure in cloud infrastructure.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/24/microsoft-agensh-multi-agent-harness-1024-agents/

🔹 Economists Report Zero Financial Payoff from AI for Most Companies
 ↳ What happened: A new analysis from Apollo Global Management reveals that despite huge corporate spending on artificial intelligence, non-tech companies are seeing virtually no financial return. While tech giants selling AI infrastructure profit, enterprise adoption has not yet translated into measurable earnings growth.
 ↳ Why it matters: Engineering teams must focus on clear productivity metrics and workflow integration rather than implementing AI for hype alone.
 ↳ Read story: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/25/ai-delivers-zero-financial-payoff-to-most-companies-according-to-apollo-economist/

🔹 Anthropic Releases Free AI Vulnerability Scanner for Open Source
 ↳ What happened: Anthropic launched OSS Scanner, a free tool that runs frontier models to detect security flaws in critical open-source repositories. The system automatically inspects codebases for potential exploits and helps maintainers fix vulnerabilities before attackers find them.
 ↳ Why it matters: Automated, AI-driven security checks can dramatically reduce open-source supply chain risks for software developers.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/25/anthropic-launches-oss-scanner-a-free-ai-vulnerability-scanner/

My take:
 ↳ Scaling AI is moving from raw model size to practical systems architecture and security. Success isn't just about spending money on models—it's about building reliable, decentralized infrastructure that actually saves time and secures software.
