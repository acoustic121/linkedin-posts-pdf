Today in AI & Tech 🔥
Tech giants are rewriting legacy codebases, automating drive-thrus, and facing a massive market reality check on AI infrastructure costs.

🔹 Cisco gives engineer unlimited AI tokens to rewrite 98% of the company's code
 ↳ What happened: Cisco handed a 27-year-old engineer unlimited AI tokens and a 24-month timeline to rewrite almost all of the company's legacy software code. This bold experiment tests whether generative AI can single-handedly modernize massive, aging enterprise architectures.
 ↳ Why it matters: It signals a dramatic shift in how enterprises plan to tackle technical debt using frontier coding models.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/cisco-gives-27-year-old-engineer-unlimited-ai-tokens-to-rewrite-98-percent-of-the-companys-legacy-code-in-24-months-president-plucks-former-startup-founder-telling-him-to-go-nuts

🔹 US tech stocks see sharp falls over AI cost fears
 ↳ What happened: Wall Street experienced sharp pullbacks in major US tech stocks as investors grew increasingly anxious over sky-high AI infrastructure spending. Markets are starting to question when the massive capital outlays for data centers and compute will actually yield profitable returns.
 ↳ Why it matters: Infrastructure costs and ROI pressure are becoming the primary bottleneck for corporate AI adoption.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvwy72e0n72yo

🔹 Starbucks wants to use AI to show you a ‘personalized digital menu’ at the drive-thru
 ↳ What happened: Starbucks announced plans to introduce AI-driven personalized digital menus at drive-thru locations. The system will dynamically adapt recommendations based on factors like time of day, weather, and customer preferences during order placement.
 ↳ Why it matters: Consumer-facing applications are shifting from static recommendation engines to real-time generative interfaces.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1023440/starbucks-ai-personalized-menu-drive-thru

My take:
 ↳ We are watching a fascinating tension play out: companies are giving engineers unlimited tools to build faster, while financial markets demand proof that the massive infrastructure bills are actually sustainable.
