Today in AI & Tech 🔥: Hardware stacks are unifying, platforms are fighting synthetic spam, and legacy enterprise code gets an AI upgrade.

🔹 Google and Meta Open-Source TorchTPU
 ↳ What happened: Google and Meta have teamed up to release TorchTPU, a native PyTorch backend designed specifically for Cloud TPUs. It runs PyTorch code natively in eager mode and integrates directly with torch.compile and XLA. This effectively replaces the older PyTorch/XLA setup for modern model training.
 ↳ Why it matters: Engineers running large-scale workloads on TPUs can now use familiar PyTorch workflows without painful translation layers or platform lock-in.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/20/google-torchtpu-open-source-native-pytorch-tpu/

🔹 Spotify Purges 50 Million AI Slop Tracks
 ↳ What happened: Spotify confirmed it has scrubbed over 50 million AI-generated spam and low-quality songs from user recommendations over the last year. The music streamer is taking aggressive steps to protect its algorithmic feeds from being drowned out by automated synthetic uploads.
 ↳ Why it matters: Generative output is becoming cheap and infinite, making data curation and content filtering the true competitive edge for tech platforms.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1014284/spotify-ai-slop-spam-songs-removed

🔹 OpenAI Targets Legacy Code and COBOL Migration
 ↳ What happened: OpenAI announced new research and benchmarks designed to boost ChatGPT's performance on low-resource programming languages and legacy enterprise stacks like COBOL. The models are being tailored for difficult enterprise tasks, including legacy system migration and software engineering evaluation.
 ↳ Why it matters: Modernizing legacy enterprise systems in banking and logistics is finally getting dedicated AI tools rather than one-size-fits-all prompts.
 ↳ Read story: https://openai.com/index/low-resource-programming-languages

My take:
 ↳ The AI ecosystem is rapidly shifting from surface-level generation to deep infrastructure work. Whether it is cleaning up recommendation algorithms or making cloud TPUs standard PyTorch targets, engineering pragmatism is taking the front seat.
