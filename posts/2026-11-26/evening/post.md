Today in AI & Tech 🔥

The pace of AI infrastructure updates and open-source tooling is moving faster than ever.

🔹 Nvidia Pauses Chip Shipments to Southeast Asia
 ↳ What happened: Nvidia has instructed its manufacturing partners to temporarily halt server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia. This move comes as the U.S. government tightens trade regulations to prevent high-performance AI chips from being rerouted into China.
 ↳ Why it matters: Global hardware supply chains remain deeply sensitive to geopolitical shifts, which directly impacts enterprise cloud capacity planning.
 ↳ Read story: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/26/nvidia-hits-pause-on-chip-shipments-to-southeast-asia/

🔹 ByteDance Open-Sources Laminar: An Async RL Framework
 ↳ What happened: Researchers from HKU and ByteDance Seed have released Laminar, an open-source asynchronous reinforcement learning post-training framework. It successfully decouples rollout, training, and weight synchronization to dramatically boost model coding capabilities.
 ↳ Why it matters: Efficient open-source training frameworks lower the barrier for teams looking to fine-tune advanced code-generation models locally.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/24/bytedance-seed-laminar-async-rl-post-training-framework/

🔹 42 State AGs Block Character.AI Data Sale
 ↳ What happened: A coalition of 42 state attorneys general is actively intervening in Character.AI's Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings. They are fighting to block the company from monetizing and selling sensitive user conversation logs to third parties.
 ↳ Why it matters: User data privacy remains a critical battleground, establishing strict precedents for what happens to personal AI chat histories when a startup goes under.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/policy/1042620/character-ai-sale-state-ags-block-selling-users-chats

My take:
 ↳ As infrastructure engineers, we are seeing a clear bifurcation: hardware supply chains are tightening globally, while open-source training pipelines are becoming remarkably accessible.
