Today in AI & Tech 🔥
From secure enterprise APIs to AI-driven infrastructure threats and "vibe-coding" with your team, here are today's top updates!

🔹 OpenAI Zero Data Retention
 ↳ What happened: OpenAI has committed to Zero Data Retention for eligible API customers and previewed Private Safety Processing. This ensures that sensitive business inputs are neither stored nor used to train future iterations of their models.
 ↳ Why it matters: This removes a massive compliance and privacy roadblock for developers building AI products in highly regulated industries like finance and healthcare.
 ↳ Read story: https://openai.com/index/offering-zero-data-retention-for-frontier-models

🔹 Slack's New "Vibe-Coding" Channels
 ↳ What happened: Slack is introducing "Slack Code," dedicated collaborative channels where teams can vibe-code together with AI agents. Instead of switching back and forth between tools, teams can now brainstorm, build, and debug in a single chat space.
 ↳ Why it matters: This shifts AI-assisted development from a solitary, single-player experience inside an IDE into a multiplayer team sport.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/tech/982628/slack-code-vibe-coding-channels-launch

🔹 AI-Powered Cyberattacks on Water Systems
 ↳ What happened: US security agencies warned that hackers are using AI tools to target internet-connected Siemens controllers in critical water facilities. These AI-assisted attacks make it much easier for hackers to scan for vulnerabilities and exploit legacy hardware.
 ↳ Why it matters: As operations engineers, this is a stark reminder that securing legacy hardware and operational technology is just as critical as securing cloud APIs.
 ↳ Read story: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/us-says-hackers-are-targeting-vulnerable-water-systems-with-the-help-of-ai/

My take:
 ↳ AI is rapidly moving in two opposite directions: boosting collaborative productivity on one hand, while drastically lowering the technical barrier for sophisticated cyberattacks on physical infrastructure on the other. Comprehensive security is no longer optional.
