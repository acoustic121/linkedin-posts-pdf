Today in AI & Tech 🔥
Finding bugs is now cheap, but human verification is hitting a major bottleneck.

🔹 Postgres 19 Delayed as AI Reviewers Flood Maintainers with Bugs
 ↳ What happened: The release of PostgreSQL 19 has been pushed back after automated AI code review tools flagged hundreds of valid edge-case bugs across the codebase. While the tools proved exceptional at finding obscure flaws, the core human maintainers lack the bandwidth to triage and fix them all before release. Finding defects is now practically free, but fixing them remains expensive.
 ↳ Why it matters: Automated code scanners will increasingly stall production release cycles unless teams scale up engineering capacity to actually resolve what AI uncovers.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theregister.com/databases/2026/10/16/postgres-19-delayed-after-ai-reviewers-find-more-bugs-than-humans-can-fix/5303471

🔹 90% of US Working Hours Miss Current AI Benchmarks
 ↳ What happened: A new comprehensive labor study reveals that roughly 90% of hours worked across the US economy involve tasks entirely missing from popular AI benchmarks. While models compete heavily on coding puzzles and academic tests, real enterprise work centers on ambiguous workflows, legacy tooling, and cross-team coordination. As a result, benchmark gains don't translate directly into workforce productivity.
 ↳ Why it matters: Measuring LLMs solely on synthetic tests creates a false sense of readiness for messy, real-world operational environments.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.mercor.com/research/labor-index/benchmark-map/

🔹 Websites Begin Locking Out Autonomous AI Agents
 ↳ What happened: Major web platforms are actively updating robots.txt files, blocking automated headless browsers, and adding friction against autonomous AI agents. While tech giants pitch agents that can browse, book services, and scrape data on user command, web publishers are pushing back to protect traffic and server infrastructure. The open web is rapidly erecting digital walls against uninvited automated bot traffic.
 ↳ Why it matters: Cloud and platform teams must prepare for strict API gating and aggressive rate-limiting as open-web agent scraping collapses.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1013195/ai-agents-blocked-web

My take:
 ↳ Generating code and dispatching agents is effortless today, but our infrastructure bottlenecks are shifting to verification, triage, and boundary control.
