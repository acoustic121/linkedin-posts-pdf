**Topic:** AI & Tech News Digest

Today in AI & Tech 🔥: Apple clamps down on AI agent disk access, OpenAI details the GPT-6 architecture roadmap, and safety debates flare up again.

🔹 Apple Limits Mac Disk Access Over AI Agent Risks
 ↳ What happened: Apple is introducing new restrictions on Mac "full disk access" permissions in response to autonomous AI agents. The company stated that agentic tools with broad system access substantially heighten data exposure and integrity risks. The new controls enforce granular permission gates whenever autonomous agents interact with local storage.
 ↳ Why it matters: Developers building local desktop agents will need to design around strict least-privilege security sandboxes.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1004295/apple-limit-mac-disk-access-ai-agents

🔹 OpenAI Shares Production Guide for GPT-6 Models
 ↳ What happened: OpenAI published an operational guide for developers looking to deploy the GPT-6 model family in production. The guide outlines how to balance reasoning effort, coordinate complex tools, and tune prompt skills for agentic execution. It aims to help teams transition safely from simple LLM wrappers to resilient autonomous workflows.
 ↳ Why it matters: Mastering runtime tool orchestration and reasoning trade-offs is now essential for running enterprise AI reliably.
 ↳ Read story: https://openai.com/index/practical-guide-building-gpt-6

🔹 OpenAI Safety Lead Resigns, Warning of Broken Culture
 ↳ What happened: David Robinson, who wrote the safety reports accompanying OpenAI's major model releases, has resigned and spoken out publicly. He warned that commercial pressure is overtaking safety protocols and that the organization's internal culture is failing to keep pace with model risks. His exit marks another prominent departure from OpenAI's technical safety division.
 ↳ Why it matters: Expect enterprise compliance teams to demand independent safety audits rather than relying on vendor claims alone.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1004408/openai-safety-quits-sounding-the-alarm

My take:
 ↳ As agents gain native OS access and complex reasoning, security boundaries can no longer be an afterthought. Cloud and platform engineers must treat agent permissions with the same zero-trust rigor as production root access.
