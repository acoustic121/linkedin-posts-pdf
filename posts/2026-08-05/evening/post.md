Today in AI & Tech 🔥 
The infrastructure and hardware landscape is shifting faster than ever as companies race to power the AI boom.

🔹 AMD's data center business is booming while gaming takes a backseat
 ↳ What happened: Driven by exploding demand for AI infrastructure, AMD's data center revenue more than doubled year-over-year in its latest earnings report, hitting $6.7 billion. This massive 107% jump highlights how compute power has completely eclipsed traditional gaming markets for chipmakers.
 ↳ Why it matters: Hardware priorities are shifting hard toward data center scale to support heavy AI workloads.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/tech/975381/amd-q2-2026-earnings-ai-gaming-ryzen

🔹 Anthropic is hiring an AI chip design team
 ↳ What happened: Following in the footsteps of other tech giants, Anthropic is building a dedicated team to design its own custom AI silicon. The goal is to co-design hardware and models directly to make future systems run significantly faster and more efficiently.
 ↳ Why it matters: Owning the silicon stack is becoming essential for frontier AI labs to cut costs and boost performance.
 ↳ Read story: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/05/anthropic-is-hiring-an-ai-chip-design-team/

🔹 SpaceX made more revenue as an AI company than a space company
 ↳ What happened: SpaceX's AI-related revenue grew more than three times to $2.6 billion over the past year. This staggering financial shift was largely driven by lucrative deals to provide heavy compute infrastructure and cloud resources to other AI companies.
 ↳ Why it matters: Unconventional companies are leveraging excess capacity and capital to become major players in the AI neocloud space.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/science/975335/spacex-made-more-money-as-a-neocloud

My take:
 ↳ The real bottleneck in AI isn't algorithms anymore—it's raw compute and power. When space companies and chip designers pivot entirely to infrastructure, you know the physical layer is where the real game is being played.
