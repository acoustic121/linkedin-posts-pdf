Today in AI & Tech 🔥
Agentic workflows are scaling up, weather forecasting is getting a physics-and-ML upgrade, and even Linus Torvalds is dipping his toes into LLM-assisted patches.

🔹 Linus Torvalds's first LLM-assisted patch series
 ↳ What happened: Linus Torvalds has submitted his first patch series utilizing LLMs for assistance, marking a major milestone for AI integration into core open-source projects. Even the most conservative maintainers are beginning to experiment with how AI tools can assist in writing and organizing code.
 ↳ Why it matters: If core Linux development is finding practical uses for LLM assistants, your engineering team should seriously evaluate how to safely integrate them into your codebases.
 ↳ Read story: https://lwn.net/Articles/1100449/

🔹 Microsoft has a new machine learning tool to improve weather forecasts
 ↳ What happened: Microsoft researchers have combined traditional physics-based models with machine learning to solve the notorious two-week breakdown window in weather forecasting. The new tool maintains high reliability far beyond the usual predictive limits.
 ↳ Why it matters: Hybrid ML-physics models are proving that AI can augment complex scientific calculations, opening doors for predictive reliability in massive infrastructure and operations.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/science/1011127/ai-weather-forecast-microsoft

🔹 Amp puts AI agents to work beyond code with OpenAI models
 ↳ What happened: OpenAI announced Amp, leveraging advanced models like GPT-6 Astra to push agent capabilities far beyond simple coding assistants and into complex, multi-step enterprise workflows. These agents are designed to run for extended periods and handle non-engineering operational tasks.
 ↳ Why it matters: As agents shift from narrow code helpers to end-to-end automation workers, cloud and DevOps engineers need to prepare for managing autonomous agent infrastructure.
 ↳ Read story: https://openai.com/index/amp-beyond-coding
