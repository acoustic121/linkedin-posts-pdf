Today in AI & Tech 🔥 
The pace of AI releases and regulatory scrutiny is shifting faster than ever.

🔹 Google announces Gemini 4 and says it’s so capable that only ‘trusted cyber defenders’ can have it right now
 ↳ What happened: Google introduced its newest frontier model, Gemini 4 Argon, designed for complex real-world software and enterprise workflows. Due to its advanced capabilities, Google is restricting early access strictly to vetted cyber defenders.
 ↳ Why it matters: High-capability frontier models pose dual-use security risks, pushing tech giants to gatekeep powerful codebases until safer deployment practices mature.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1002980/google-gemini-4-argon

🔹 FTC is investigating OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI companies over product risks
 ↳ What happened: Federal regulators have launched a broad formal probe into top AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic regarding potential consumer and product risks. The investigation targets safety practices, market dominance, and deployment oversight.
 ↳ Why it matters: Regulatory bodies are stepping up active enforcement, meaning compliance and risk management will soon be core requirements for any team deploying LLMs.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/ftc-ai-probe-openai-anthropic.html

🔹 Introducing Olmo-core 3: Open, scalable training infrastructure for large MoEs
 ↳ What happened: Allen AI has released Olmo-core 3 on Hugging Face, bringing an open and highly scalable training infrastructure specifically built for Large Mixture of Experts (MoE) models.
 ↳ Why it matters: Democratizing scalable training for MoE models gives open-source developers better tools to build and fine-tune efficient, high-performance architectures.
 ↳ Read story: https://huggingface.co/blog/allenai/olmocore3
