Today in AI & Tech 🔥 Navigating the shift from quick code prototypes to strict regulations and real-world benchmarks.

🔹 Supabase Releases Evals for Coding Agents
 ↳ What happened: Supabase has open-sourced an evaluation framework to benchmark coding agents like Claude Code, Codex, and OpenCode on actual database tasks like building schemas and debugging. Instead of generic tests, it measures how well these AI tools handle real-world backend engineering workflows.
 ↳ Why it matters: Generic benchmarks are dying; developers now need domain-specific evals to trust AI in production environments.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/01/supabase-releases-evals-an-open-source-benchmark-that-scores-claude-code-codex-and-opencode-on-real-supabase-tasks/

🔹 AI Doesn't Generate Working Products, That's Still Your Job
 ↳ What happened: A viral discussion on Hacker News highlights the massive gap between generating a quick AI prototype and building a secure, scalable product. While AI can write initial boilerplate quickly, architecture, edge cases, and deployment remain entirely human responsibilities.
 ↳ Why it matters: Engineering teams must stop treating AI prototypes as production-ready code to avoid costly outages down the line.
 ↳ Read story: https://weeraman.com/the-prototype-isnt-the-product/

🔹 EU Mandates Labels on Authentic-Looking AI Content
 ↳ What happened: The European Union is rolling out strict regulations requiring clear labeling on authentic-looking AI-generated content to combat deepfakes and misinformation. Platforms and creators operating in the EU must now ensure synthetic media is transparently marked.
 ↳ Why it matters: Compliance is becoming a mandatory part of the software deployment lifecycle for any AI-powered application touching European users.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.engadget.com/2227966/eu-mandate-labels-on-authentic-looking-ai-content/

My take:
 ↳ The industry is rapidly moving past the hype phase into an era of strict accountability, where governance, proper testing, and production reality checks matter more than flashy demos.
