Today in AI & Tech 🔥 OpenAI drops GPT-6 Astra, sub-$1k LLM training becomes reality, and music giants embrace AI remixes.

🔹 OpenAI Releases GPT-6 Astra
 ↳ What happened: OpenAI announced GPT-6 Astra, built specifically for advanced enterprise work. The model introduces multi-step reasoning, native computer-use capabilities, and refined judgment for design and code. It is designed to navigate applications and execute workflow automation directly.
 ↳ Why it matters: Developers gain access to highly capable autonomous agents that can interact directly with desktop and web applications.
 ↳ Read story: https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra-next-generation-work

🔹 Training a 3.8B LLM for $998
 ↳ What happened: An independent developer successfully trained a 3.8B parameter language model to a strong benchmark score for under $1,000. By optimizing dataset quality and GPU scheduling, the project demonstrates that training performant models is getting drastically cheaper.
 ↳ Why it matters: Small engineering teams can now train custom, domain-specific models in-house without massive cloud budgets.
 ↳ Read story: https://hugovergnes.github.io/little-lm-3-8b/

🔹 Universal Music Partners with ElevenLabs for AI Remixes
 ↳ What happened: Universal Music Group is launching a new AI platform with ElevenLabs that lets users create remixes and mashups using UMG's licensed catalog. This marks a shift from music labels fighting generative AI to actively commercializing it.
 ↳ Why it matters: Copyright-compliant AI workflows are becoming mainstream, giving creators legal access to premium audio assets.
 ↳ Read story: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/993465/universal-music-elevenlabs-ai

My take:
 ↳ AI model training costs are falling rapidly just as top-tier models gain real workspace autonomy. As licensing models settle legal doubts, agentic automation is quickly shifting from experiment to production reality.
